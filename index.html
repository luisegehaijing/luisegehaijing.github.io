I am a Computer Science PhD candidate at Washington University in St. Louis, coadvised by Prof. Yevgeniy Vorobeychik and Prof. Gregory Kehne. My current research interests cover the foundations of AI (e.g., integrating learning and reasoning, and automated discovery) and collective decision making (e.g., AI alignment, and computational social choice).

I started out as a cognitive science student at the University of Edinburgh, with the passion to figure out how the mind works. While this curiosity has never diminished, it just happened that the beauty of maths struck me greatly. I ended up getting an MSc in pure maths at Imperial College London under my amazing advisor Prof. Paolo Cascini. My master's thesis was on algebraic geometry, inspired by a fantastic idea called "the periodic table of shapes". I have turned back to CS due to both its philosophical considerations and societal impact. Outside of research, you may find me reading, writing, watching films and plays, making art, or traveling.

If you are an undergraduate or master student interested in research or you are a researcher interested in collaboration, please don't hesitate to contact me!

Misc

Here are a few more topics I think I will be passionate about for life, and my recent attempts and thoughts under their umbrella:

  1. Social entrepreneurship. I recently created an app called Synchria, and I am still figuring out how to build communities. In general, I believe there is a huge opportunity out there to help people connect with each other, whether for friendship, relationships, collaboration, or just helping each other out. The visible part is the website and social media, but there is an invisible part that is messier, more complicated, and even more important. I believe we now have good enough technology, including better search, to create a more spontaneous, effective, and heart-to-heart channel.
  2. Philosophy of science and maths. The recent shake-ups between AI and maths are definitely shocking, but in general I still feel optimistic, not hostile, towards the technology itself. That said, I fully agree with Prof. Tarik Aougab's belief that maths needs humans and humans need maths (check out this amazing guide for maths students). My short essay will also appear on Proofs & Prompts soon! Here is its abstract:
    “All the relationships are emotional,” shouted a stranger on the street. Turns out that's true even for mathematics. Emotion may be one of the most human things we have. Judging by what AI still can't do, perhaps we should cherish this irrational part more.
